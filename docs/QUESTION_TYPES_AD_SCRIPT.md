# Question Types ad — voice-over script

For the capture produced by `scripts/render_question_showcase.sh`
(`artifacts/demo/question-types-showcase.mp4`). Exact timings for the cut you
are working to are written next to the video as
`question-types-showcase.cues.md` — re-read that file after any re-render,
because a change to the pacing moves every cue.

## Casting

**Two voices, not one.** The paper deliberately runs from Year 4 work (long
division, the factor ladder) to senior work (sketching a parabola, naming its
axis of symmetry). One child cannot plausibly narrate both ends of that, and an
ad that asks them to will sound staged — which is the one thing a student-voice
ad cannot afford.

| Voice | Age | Carries |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **YOUNGER** | 9–11 | Opening, and question types 1–10 |
| **OLDER** | 15–16 | Types 11–13, where the maths is senior |
| **YOUNGER** | | The last two lines, so it closes where it opened |

If you only have one voice, record the YOUNGER lines and cut scenes 11–13 from
the video — do not have a ten-year-old say "axis of symmetry".

## Tone

Talking to a friend about an app they actually use, not reading a brochure.
Unimpressed rather than delighted: the strongest version of this is a kid who
finds it *normal* that the app takes their working, because that is the claim.
Let lines land short of the next cue — silence over the screen recording is
fine, wall-to-wall talking is not.

New Zealand English throughout: "maths", "colour".

**Gap** is the seconds until the next cue — the room a line has to breathe in.
At a natural child's pace of about 2.5 words a second, leave a second and a
half of air, so a 6-second gap takes roughly eleven words. Every line above
fits its gap with room to spare; if a take runs long, cut words rather than
crowd the next cue.

Each scene's caption card appears at the cue and names the question type. The
voice should never say the same words — let the caption do the labelling and
use the line for what the caption cannot say.

### The three lines that do the selling

If anything gets cut, keep these:

1. **"Not one of them is multiple choice."** — the whole pitch in six words.
2. **"Other apps just want the answer. This one takes my working."** — names
   the competitor gap without naming a competitor.
3. **"Four right out of five gets me four out of five."** — partial credit, in
   a child's words.

## Short cut (about 45 seconds)

Most ad placements want under a minute. Keep the opening, four question types
that cannot be done on paper-to-photo, and the result.

| Keep | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Sign-in + homework list | Establishes a real classroom, not a quiz toy |
| Long division | The strongest single differentiator |
| Measure an angle | The protractor is the most visual thing in the video |
| Plot a line | Interaction a worksheet cannot do at all |
| Sketch a graph | Shows the range goes up to senior maths |
| Result, 13 / 13 | The payoff |

## Recording notes

- Record each line as its own take, with a couple of seconds of silence around
  it. Lines get moved when the edit changes; one long take does not.
- Get at least three takes of the three selling lines above.
- A quiet carpeted room beats a good microphone in a bare one. A duvet behind
  the speaker kills most of the echo.
- Do not direct the child to sound excited. Ask them to explain it to a friend
  who has not seen it.
- Keep one clean take of room tone with nobody speaking, for the editor to
  patch gaps with.
- The on-screen captions already say what each question type is, so the voice
  should not repeat them. Where a line and a caption say the same thing, cut
  the line.

## Consent

The voices belong to real children. Get written parental permission for use in
advertising before the ad goes anywhere public, and keep it on file — the same
release you would need for a photo. If the voices are your own students, that
permission is separate from anything they signed to use the app.
